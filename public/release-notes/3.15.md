# v3.15 — Dual licensing 📜

The project now has formal licensing. No code change, no user-facing behavior change — just paperwork that matters for anyone forking, contributing, or considering a commercial license.

## What's new

- **`LICENSE`** — full canonical AGPL-3.0-or-later text (FSF-published, unmodified) with a copyright header naming Scott T Weaver as the copyright holder.
- **`LICENSING.md`** — the entry-point guide. Plain table mapping "your situation" to "which license you need."
- **`COMMERCIAL-LICENSE.md`** — plain-English summary of the commercial license option (annual term, what you get, what it doesn't grant) and contact info for inquiries.
- **`COMMERCIAL-TERMS.md`** — substantive terms outline (parties, grant, term, restrictions, warranties). Marked as a placeholder until reviewed by legal counsel; real customer agreements will be more formal documents.
- **`CLA.md`** — Individual Contributor License Agreement for personal contributions.
- **`CLA-CORPORATE.md`** — Corporate variant for contributions made by employees on company time.
- **`README.md`** — top-level note + new "Licensing & contributing" section pointing at all of the above.
- **`package.json`** — `"license": "AGPL-3.0-or-later OR SEE LICENSE IN COMMERCIAL-LICENSE.md"` plus `"author"` field.

## Why dual-license?

AGPL keeps modifications open — anyone running a modified version as a network service has to release their source. Good default for an open project. The commercial alternative gives companies that can't accept AGPL terms (closed-source policies, embedded use cases) a paid path so they can build on top legally. The CLA is what makes the dual-license model possible: contributors grant the project owner enough rights to offer commercial sub-licenses on contributed code, while retaining their own copyright.

## Legal review caveat

The AGPL text in `LICENSE` is canonical and safe. The commercial license outline and the CLAs are modeled on widely-used patterns (Apache ICLA, Plausible, Cal.com, Sentry) but **have not been reviewed by legal counsel**. They're a reasonable starting point for casual review and as the basis of an actual agreement, not legal advice. For binding use — commercial license negotiations, contributor disputes — get a lawyer.
